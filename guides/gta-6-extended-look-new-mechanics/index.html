GTA 6 Extended Look Details: New Mechanics Confirmed

GTA 6 Extended Look details reveal a new wanted system, car theft, combat, phone apps, Criminal Profile, activities, maps and more.

GTA 6 Extended Look Details: The Biggest New Mechanics Confirmed

The GTA 6 Extended Look details go far beyond the first wave of obvious reveals. A day after Rockstar released its roughly 26-minute showcase on August 27, 2026, players and preview outlets had picked through the PlayStation 5 footage frame by frame, uncovering deeper systems for police pursuits, vehicle theft, combat, phones, character relationships, activities and player behavior.

The biggest takeaway is that Grand Theft Auto VI is layering several interconnected systems beneath its familiar open-world formula. Police remember what suspects look like, stealing parked cars can require specialized tools, Jason and Lucia can operate together or separately, and a new Criminal Profile tracks what kind of criminals they become.

GTA 6 Has a Much Smarter Wanted System

The police overhaul is significantly deeper than it first appears.

Rather than automatically giving the player a wanted level whenever an offense occurs, GTA VI tracks how authorities learn about the crime.

Witnesses Can Report Crimes

Witnesses are central to the new system. Civilians who see illegal activity can pull out a phone and attempt to report what happened.

That means committing an offense without being observed may allow Jason or Lucia to avoid immediate police attention.

However, the simple statement that you "only" become wanted if a witness calls the police is too absolute. Preview coverage indicates that alarms, direct police observation and other detection methods can also alert law enforcement.

In practice, the system appears to work more like this:

  • Commit a crime without being detected and police may never be alerted.
  • A civilian witness can report what they saw.
  • Security or vehicle alarms can also attract attention.
  • Police can react immediately if they directly observe the offense.

This creates a stronger stealth element around ordinary crime.

Police Remember Your Description

Getting into another vehicle does not automatically erase police awareness.

Authorities can retain identifying information about a suspect, including details such as:

  • Face.
  • Clothing.
  • Vehicle.
  • Whether Jason and Lucia were seen together.

That means escaping is less about simply reaching another car and more about breaking line of sight and removing the identifiers police are searching for.

The Extended Look demonstrates this during a getaway in which Lucia destroys a vehicle with a Molotov before moving on.

Changing vehicles can still help, but it is only one stage of losing the police.

Splitting Jason and Lucia Up Can Reduce Police Awareness

The dual-protagonist system introduces another escape tactic.

Preview reporting indicates that when Jason and Lucia are identified as traveling together, splitting them up can reduce the effectiveness of the police description and may lower the active wanted level by one star in applicable circumstances.

This is a particularly interesting use of GTA VI's two-character structure. Instead of character switching existing only for mission variety, the fact that Jason and Lucia can separate becomes part of the wanted-system strategy.

For a closer look at its icons and identifiers, see our GTA 6 HUD and wanted system guide.

The Six-Star Wanted Level Returns

GTA VI restores the classic six-star wanted scale, replacing GTA V's five-star maximum.

Four stars are visible during the Extended Look footage, while preview reporting confirms that the meter extends to six.

Exactly what forces appear at five and six stars has not yet been fully detailed by Rockstar.

The reworked crime and combat systems in action.

Car Theft Has Been Completely Reworked

Stealing parked vehicles is no longer necessarily as simple as pressing a button and driving away.

The GTA 6 car theft system introduces different entry methods depending on the type and security of the vehicle.

Older Cars Can Be Opened With a Slim Jim

Jason is shown using a slim jim to gain access to an older vehicle.

This provides a relatively discreet method of opening suitable cars without immediately smashing a window and making noise.

Newer Cars May Require a Key Cloner

More modern vehicles can require additional technology.

Preview coverage describes a key cloner that can be used on newer cars whose electronic security prevents the simpler slim-jim approach.

That effectively gives different vehicle generations different levels of theft difficulty.

Smashing the Window Still Works

Players who do not want to spend time bypassing a lock can use the traditional solution: break the window.

The trade-off is obvious.

Smashing glass creates noise and can trigger the car's alarm, increasing the chance that witnesses or police notice what is happening.

Classic Carjacking Is Still Available

None of this removes traditional Grand Theft Auto carjacking.

If a vehicle is already occupied, Jason or Lucia can still forcibly remove the driver and take it.

The expanded tools mainly apply to stealing parked and unattended cars.

WAINK Lets You Scan Cars Before Stealing Them

One of the phone applications identified in preview coverage is called WAINK.

The app can be used to scan a vehicle and provide useful information before the player decides whether it is worth stealing.

Reported information includes details such as:

  • Vehicle value.
  • Security or lock information.
  • Tracking technology.

This is especially useful if vehicle theft is being performed for profit rather than simply transportation.

Instead of stealing random cars, players can assess whether a target is valuable enough to justify the additional risk.

Some Cars Have Trackers

Certain vehicles can contain tracking systems.

A tracked car gives law enforcement another way to follow the player and can turn what looked like an easy theft into a more serious pursuit.

That creates another risk-versus-reward decision: an expensive vehicle may be worth more money, but its security and tracking technology can also make it harder to steal successfully.

GTA 6 Combat Has a Max Payne Influence

The Extended Look also gives the clearest demonstration yet of GTA VI's redesigned gunplay.

Its cover shooting still has similarities to GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2, but one of the most notable additions evokes Rockstar's Max Payne 3.

Focus Mode Slows Down Combat

A Focus-style targeting ability can slow the action and highlight vulnerable areas on enemies.

During the footage, different body zones appear with red and yellow targeting indicators.

The colors appear to distinguish between potentially lethal and disabling areas.

Examples include:

  • Red highlights around vital regions such as the head or chest.
  • Yellow highlights around joints, limbs or other areas that may incapacitate rather than immediately kill.

The system gives players more control over how they deal with armed opponents.

Enemies Can Be Incapacitated

GTA VI's location-based damage allows players to shoot specific parts of an enemy's body.

Targeting an arm or leg can potentially disable or incapacitate an opponent rather than simply killing them.

This opens the door to encounters where lethal force is not always required.

Weapons Can Potentially Be Shot From an Enemy's Hands

Targeting highlights also appear around hands and weapons, with preview interpretations suggesting that players may be able to disarm opponents by shooting at the weapon or the hand holding it.

The Extended Look clearly establishes more precise body-part targeting, although the exact rules determining when a weapon can be shot away have not been formally documented by Rockstar.

Non-Lethal Approaches Are Possible

The footage and preview information point toward a more deliberate distinction between lethal and non-lethal combat.

Players may be able to:

  • Shoot limbs to incapacitate.
  • Target vital areas for lethal shots.
  • Disable certain enemies instead of killing them.
  • Use situational melee options.

This also becomes relevant to the game's Criminal Profile, which evaluates more than simply whether the player committed an illegal act.

Jason and Lucia Have Different Combat Leanings

Preview material also describes different weapon preferences for the protagonists.

Jason is presented as being particularly comfortable with weapons such as pistols and assault rifles, fitting his military background.

Lucia is frequently shown using shotguns and close-range firepower, including a sequence where she handles a shotgun one-handed while carrying a bag.

These should be understood as character and gameplay leanings rather than strict class restrictions. Rockstar has not announced that either protagonist is locked out of particular weapon categories.

The GTA 6 Phone Is a Physical Object

Phones are no longer presented only as flat interface overlays.

When Jason or Lucia uses a phone, the character physically holds the device while the camera moves closer to the screen, integrating it into the world as a three-dimensional object.

Messages and interface elements can then appear around the phone while the character remains visible.

This helps blur the line between normal gameplay and menu interactions.

GTA 6 Phone Apps Include Snapmatic, WAINK and Ryde Me

Several applications have appeared in Extended Look footage or associated preview coverage.

Snapmatic Returns

Rockstar's parody photography and social-media platform Snapmatic returns.

Players can still use it for photography and interact with the game's wider social-media ecosystem.

Because Jason and Lucia are criminals trying to avoid unwanted attention, preview information suggests their ability to publicly upload personal photographs may be more restricted than GTA V's implementation.

Livestreaming Includes a Live Chat

The game's social-media satire extends to livestreaming.

One memorable sequence involves a character broadcasting events while inside a vehicle, complete with a scrolling live-chat feed appearing alongside the stream.

The mechanic demonstrates that social media in GTA VI is not limited to prerecorded parody clips. Live events occurring around Jason and Lucia can become content inside the game world.

Ryde Me Provides Taxi Transportation

The Ryde Me app is associated with GTA VI's taxi service.

Preview coverage indicates that it can be used to arrange rides and facilitate faster travel across Leonida, expanding the familiar taxi transport system through a modern rideshare-style interface.

WAINK Helps With Vehicle Theft

As described earlier, WAINK can inspect potential vehicle targets before theft.

That connects the phone directly to one of GTA VI's criminal systems rather than leaving it purely as a communications device.

Jason and Lucia Can Play Together or Separately

GTA VI's dual-protagonist structure is considerably more flexible than simply switching between two separate stories.

Jason and Lucia can operate:

  • Independently.
  • Together while exploring.
  • As partners during missions.
  • In different areas of the same mission.
  • In different roles during a pursuit or robbery.

When they are together, switching can happen quickly.

When they are geographically separated, transitions are more similar to GTA V's longer character-switching sequences.

AI Controls the Character You Are Not Playing

When Jason and Lucia are working together, the protagonist you are not directly controlling can be handled by the AI.

That character can continue participating in the situation rather than standing passively in place.

Examples shown or described include:

  • Fighting alongside the player.
  • Driving.
  • Covering an area.
  • Participating in robberies.
  • Following the controlled protagonist through a location.

This allows two-person missions to function naturally without becoming co-op gameplay.

GTA VI's story mode remains a single-player experience.

You Can Contact Your Partner by Phone

Jason and Lucia can communicate through calls and text messages.

Preview reporting also indicates that players can use communication options to coordinate with the other protagonist and, in certain circumstances, bring them into activities.

Rockstar has not yet published a complete rule set explaining exactly when the partner can be summoned, so this should not be interpreted as a universal "call partner anywhere" feature.

Jason and Lucia Have a Relationship System

Their partnership is not purely mechanical.

Rockstar and preview coverage describe a dynamic relationship between Jason and Lucia that reacts to player choices.

Players can spend time together or operate independently, and their relationship develops according to how they interact.

Small details reinforce the system, including affectionate animations such as holding hands.

The relationship is tied to the story rather than existing as a disconnected romance minigame.

Rockstar North has emphasized that the studio wanted players to spend enough time with each protagonist to form a stronger connection with both characters, which helped drive the decision to use two leads instead of GTA V's three.

Safehouses Function More Like RDR2 Camps

GTA VI's home bases borrow some design ideas from Red Dead Redemption 2.

Rather than being empty locations used primarily for saving, safehouses function as places for character management and everyday activities.

Players can use them for clothing, grooming and other appearance-related systems.

The structure recalls RDR2's camps, where character upkeep and equipment management were integrated into the world rather than hidden entirely inside menus.

Gym Workouts Change Your Physique

Physical customization also returns.

Using the gym can build muscle, bringing back one of the most recognizable character-development concepts from Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas.

Jason and Lucia's bodies can visibly change depending on lifestyle.

Eating too much can contribute to weight gain, while exercise can increase muscle definition.

Some reports also associate smoking with changes to body weight, but available reliable coverage clearly confirms dietary effects more strongly than a direct smoking-to-weight mechanic. Smoking itself appears as an interaction, while the specific claim that smoking causes weight gain remains insufficiently verified.

Salons Let You Change Hairstyles

Hair salons provide more conventional cosmetic customization.

Players can change hairstyles and other elements of Jason and Lucia's appearances at dedicated locations.

Clothing Stays at Your Safehouse

Wardrobe management is tied to safehouses, giving players a persistent location for clothing and outfit changes.

Certain accessories can also be swapped more quickly during gameplay.

Weapons Can Be Stored in Vehicle Trunks

GTA VI also limits how much weaponry characters carry directly.

Additional guns and gear can be kept in vehicle trunks or storage locations.

This is another concept reminiscent of Red Dead Redemption 2, where the player did not automatically carry an entire arsenal at all times.

GTA 6 Introduces a Criminal Profile System

One of the most important systems revealed through post-showcase previews is the Criminal Profile.

It has been compared with Red Dead Redemption 2's Honor system, but Rockstar's version is more specifically concerned with what kind of criminal the player becomes.

It is not simply a morality meter measuring whether Jason and Lucia are "good" or "evil."

Criminal Profile Tracks How You Behave

Your actions can influence the profile according to the way you handle situations.

A disciplined criminal who completes a robbery cleanly may be viewed differently from someone who creates unnecessary chaos.

Examples described in preview reporting include distinctions between:

  • Professional criminal behavior.
  • Unnecessary aggression.
  • Violence against people who no longer pose a threat.
  • Self-defense.
  • Positive interactions with the world.

The system therefore evaluates context rather than simply assigning a negative value every time the player commits a crime.

NPC Reactions Can Change

Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson has explained that the world responds to the way players behave.

NPCs may treat Jason and Lucia differently depending on their actions and reputation, while behavior can also influence aspects of the narrative experience.

Rockstar has stressed that the system is not designed to prescribe one "correct" playstyle.

A low Criminal Profile does not necessarily mean someone is playing GTA VI incorrectly. It reflects the type of criminal behavior the player has chosen.

Required Story-Mission Violence Is Treated Differently

The original claim that "crimes committed during story missions don't count" needs a small qualification.

Nelson has explained that actions a mission requires the player to perform are effectively treated as fair game.

In other words, the Criminal Profile is not intended to punish the player for violence or illegal acts that the story itself forces them to commit.

That does not necessarily mean every optional action performed during every mission is ignored.

The more accurate rule is that required mission behavior should not count against the player simply because the story demanded it.

Even Petting Dogs Can Feed Into the Behavior System

GTA VI includes contextual animal interactions.

Players can pet or scold dogs, and the Extended Look includes an interface prompt associated with interacting positively with an animal.

Some coverage has described dog petting as producing "good karma." That description is useful shorthand, but Rockstar explicitly distinguishes the Criminal Profile from a simple RDR2-style morality or karma bar.

The safer conclusion is that interactions with animals are among the types of behavior the game can recognize.

And yes, you can pet the dogs.

GTA 6 Has a Huge Range of Side Activities

The Extended Look substantially expanded the known list of activities available across Leonida.

Confirmed or directly demonstrated activities include:

  • Parachuting and skydiving.
  • Base jumping from tall buildings.
  • Scuba diving.
  • Kayaking.
  • Mini golf.
  • Gym workouts.
  • Basketball.
  • Pool.
  • Street racing.
  • Dirt-bike racing.
  • Swimming.
  • Jet skiing.
  • Boating.
  • Zoo visits and animal observation.
  • Petting or scolding dogs.

Rockstar has also previously established activities such as fishing, hunting and off-roading through official location descriptions and promotional material.

For the broader catalog, see our GTA 6 activities guide.

Racing Includes Cars, Dirt Bikes and Performance Upgrades

Vehicle competition is not restricted to one style of racing.

The showcase includes or supports multiple types of motorsport, including:

  • Street racing.
  • Circuit-style car events.
  • Dirt-bike races.
  • Off-road driving.

NOS or nitrous-style performance boosts also appear in racing-related coverage, further expanding the tuning and racing side of GTA VI's vehicle systems.

The exact availability and upgrade rules for nitrous have not yet been comprehensively documented by Rockstar.

Vice City Is Divided Into Distinct Districts

Vice City is presented less like one uniform urban space and more like a collection of distinct neighborhoods and districts.

Different areas have their own architecture, population, atmosphere and identity.

That follows Rockstar's broader approach to Leonida, which includes environments far beyond Vice City itself.

The game's official world already includes locations and regions such as:

  • Vice City.
  • Leonida Keys.
  • Grassrivers.
  • Port Gellhorn.
  • Ambrosia.
  • Mount Kalaga National Park.

The Extended Look reinforces the sense that individual parts of Vice City itself will also feel meaningfully different from one another.

The Minimap Shows Buildings in 3D

Rockstar has also overhauled map presentation.

The in-game minimap can render building shapes with three-dimensional depth, making dense urban environments easier to read.

This should provide more useful spatial information than a purely flat road layout, particularly in areas with multi-level structures.

The Pause Map Uses Satellite-Style Imagery

The larger map interface has changed as well.

Instead of relying entirely on the simplified street-and-block presentation familiar from earlier GTA titles, GTA VI uses satellite-style imagery to depict Leonida.

This gives the world map a more geographically realistic appearance and makes terrain, coastlines, vegetation and urban density easier to distinguish visually.

The Extended Look Was Captured on PlayStation 5

Rockstar explicitly states that the Extended Look was captured entirely from in-game footage on PlayStation 5.

Separate preview reporting indicates that the demonstration was running on a standard or base PS5, rather than requiring a PS5 Pro.

That is notable given the density of Vice City's traffic, crowds, lighting, interiors and environmental effects.

The Footage Runs at 30fps

The published Extended Look is presented at 30 frames per second.

Rockstar North's Rob Nelson also reportedly told preview attendees that current console builds were running at 30fps.

However, Rockstar has not published a formal technical specification page guaranteeing that 30fps will be the only launch mode on every console.

No 60fps performance mode has been officially announced.

The most precise current position is:

  • The Extended Look itself is 30fps.
  • Preview attendees report the base-PS5 build running at 30fps.
  • Rockstar development leadership has reportedly described current console builds as 30fps.
  • Whether an alternative 40fps or 60fps mode will eventually exist remains unconfirmed.

Cutscenes Transition Seamlessly Into Gameplay

One of the showcase's most impressive presentation tricks is how frequently scenes shift from cinematic framing to direct player control without an obvious loading screen or hard cut.

These seamless cutscene-to-gameplay transitions make it less obvious where scripted storytelling ends and interactive gameplay begins.

Rockstar has used versions of this technique before, but the Extended Look suggests it is being pushed considerably further in GTA VI.

The Radio Returns

The traditional GTA radio system remains part of the experience.

Players can listen to music while driving, maintaining one of the series' most recognizable features.

The Extended Look also demonstrates how deeply music, television, advertising and social media are integrated into Leonida's broader satire.

The Biggest GTA 6 Mechanics Confirmed After the Extended Look

Taken together, the Extended Look itself and the wave of sanctioned preview reporting around it establish a much more systemic GTA than the initial footage alone suggested.

The most important additions include:

  • A six-star wanted system.
  • Witnesses who can report crimes.
  • Police tracking faces, clothing, vehicles and whether the protagonists are together.
  • Splitting Jason and Lucia up as an escape tactic.
  • Destroying or abandoning identifiable getaway vehicles.
  • Slim jims for stealing older cars.
  • Key cloning for newer vehicles.
  • Window smashing and vehicle alarms.
  • Vehicle tracking technology.
  • The WAINK vehicle-scanning app.
  • Traditional occupied-car carjacking.
  • Slow-motion Focus combat.
  • Body-part targeting.
  • Lethal and disabling hit zones.
  • More opportunities for non-lethal combat.
  • A fully rendered 3D phone.
  • Snapmatic.
  • Livestreaming and live chat.
  • Ryde Me taxi transportation.
  • Character switching during activities and missions.
  • AI control of Jason or Lucia when the player controls the other.
  • Calls and texts between the protagonists.
  • A dynamic Jason and Lucia relationship.
  • Safehouse-based appearance and equipment management.
  • Gym-driven muscle development.
  • Diet-related weight changes.
  • Hair salons and wardrobe customization.
  • Weapon storage in vehicle trunks.
  • The new Criminal Profile behavior system.
  • Context-sensitive NPC reactions.
  • Mission-required crimes being treated differently by the profile.
  • Dog interactions.
  • Parachuting.
  • Scuba diving.
  • Kayaking.
  • Mini golf.
  • Street and dirt-bike racing.
  • Gym activities.
  • 3D minimap buildings.
  • Satellite-style world mapping.
  • Seamless cinematics and gameplay.
  • The return of in-car radio.

Rockstar may still adjust individual systems before release, but these details provide the clearest picture yet of how much GTA VI is expanding the series' underlying simulation.

Conclusion

The GTA 6 Extended Look details reveal a game built around interconnected systems rather than isolated new features. Stealing a car can involve scanning its value, bypassing security and worrying about a tracker. Committing a crime can create witnesses, descriptions and long-term police awareness. Combat can involve targeting specific limbs, while Jason and Lucia's relationship, appearance and criminal behavior can evolve according to how they are played.

The new Criminal Profile, six-star wanted system, physical phone, expanded vehicle theft and flexible dual-protagonist mechanics are particularly significant because they influence several parts of the game at once.

Rockstar's August 27 showcase was therefore more than a visual demonstration of Vice City. Combined with the developer interviews and sanctioned previews that followed, it exposed a much deeper layer of GTA VI's design — and finer details will continue to become clearer as Rockstar approaches the game's November 19, 2026 release.
